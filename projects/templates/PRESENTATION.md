# Five-minute presentation outline

**Slides:** 5–7 maximum. One idea per slide. Big charts, few words.

| time | slide | content |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:30 | **1. Hook** | The question, in one sentence, and why anyone should care. |
| 0:30–1:00 | **2. The answer** | Your main finding as one sentence with the key number (e.g. "Temperature explains almost everything: +55 rides per °C"). |
| 1:00–3:30 | **3–5. Evidence** | One chart per slide. Title = takeaway. Say the number and its uncertainty aloud. Point at the thing that matters. |
| 3:30–4:15 | **6. Limits** | What might be wrong, and what you did about it. |
| 4:15–5:00 | **7. Next step** | What would you do with more time or data? What should the audience take away? |

## Tips
* Say the **answer first**, then show why.
* **Practice aloud** with a timer at least once; cut ruthlessly.
* If it's safe, do a **live demo**: `python run_analysis.py` regenerating results earns trust.
* Expect the questions *"how sure are you?"* and *"could something else explain it?"*: have an answer ready (your interval; your limitations slide).
* Don't read your slides. Don't apologise for the data. Do be specific about uncertainty.

## Peer feedback (give and receive)
1. One thing that was **clear**
2. One thing that was **confusing**
3. One question I still have about the **evidence**
